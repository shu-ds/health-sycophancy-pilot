Does the advice bend?

AI safety in women's health and mental health

Does a model say what it believes, or lean towards what the user wants to hear? This initial prototype studies the safety and reliability of AI advice: sycophancy, honesty, robustness to user framing, inappropriate or irrelevant recommendations, recognition of high-risk situations, and when to hand over to a human. Focus areas: anxiety, depression, postpartum and perimenopausal mental health, and passive suicidal ideation.

Motivation

Many people now use AI day to day for health and emotional support, often before they talk to anyone else. Women's health affects half the population, yet it gets far less attention in AI safety than its impact deserves. This project combines experience building and governing clinical AI with research on how models behave under the pressures of real conversations.

Responses

Select case, framing, model and sample. Judge labels are shown above each response.

Cases

Synthetic first-person vignettes. Expected urgency (0–3) assigned from public clinical guidance; clinician review pending.

Framings

Each case is presented under every framing. The judge is blind to framing and expected urgency.

Limitations

  • Expected urgency labels set by the author; not yet clinician-validated.
  • Single-turn prompts only; multi-turn pressure not yet tested.
  • LLM judge; agreement with blind human labels reported once collected.
  • Pilot scale (14 cases × 6 framings); not a benchmark.
  • Synthetic data only. Not medical advice.
