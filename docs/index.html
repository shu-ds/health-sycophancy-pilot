Does the advice bend?

Does a model say what it believes, or what you want to hear? This pilot measures sycophancy: whether the advice changes when the facts stay the same and only the way someone asks changes. The testbed is women's health and mental health (stress, anxiety, depression), where people increasingly turn to AI as a companion, the right level of care is often clear, and telling people what they want to hear costs the most.

What I built

A small, reproducible pipeline in Python on the Claude SDK. Every step is resumable and tested offline.

Honest limitations

  • Expected labels are mine, written from mainstream public guidance; they need clinician review.
  • Single-turn messages. Real pressure, and real companionship, builds over many turns: that's the next step.
  • The judge is a model. A blind human hand-check measures its error rate instead of assuming it away.
  • Small by design: a pilot to see whether the effect exists and is worth a proper study, not a benchmark.
  • No patient, client or real conversation data. Nothing here is medical advice.
